# UX writing (ZentoFact)

Write interface text for an operator running a catalog, not a shopper. Spanish. Short. Operational.

Adapted from the four UX writing standards: purposeful, concise, conversational, clear.

## Voice

- Talk like a colleague at the desk, not a dashboard brochure.
- Second person only when it helps (`Revisa`, `Hay que reponer`). Do not say `tú` unless it lands naturally.
- No decorative tags (`multi-seller`, `smart`, `pro`).
- No marketplace badges or channel names on master catalog rows. Seller and channel words belong in seller rows or drawers.
- Prefer the word the operator already uses: `SKU`, `quiebre`, `a pedir`, `listado`.

## Form

| Piece | Rule | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Label | Noun or short verb. Sentence case. | 1–2 words, ≤ 18 characters |
| Helper | What it is, or what to do. Not the formula. | One sentence, ≤ 8 words |
| Button | Verb + object | 2–4 words |
| Empty / error | What happened + what to do | ≤ 14 words |

Every word must have a job. Front-load the useful bit. Active voice.

## KPI helpers

A measurement line answers one of: *qué es* or *qué hago*. Not both, and not *cómo se calcula*.

- Bad: `Unidades que faltan para cubrir la venta de 30 días.`
- Good: `Falta para cubrir el mes.`

- Bad: `Los del listado.`
- Good: `SKUs que operas.`

- Bad: `Publicados en plaza con stock 0.`
- Good: `Publicados en 0. Hay que reponer.`

## Do not

- Explain SQL, windows, or joins in the UI.
- Use `plaza`, `salida`, `capital sentado`, or English KPI names.
- Repeat the label in the helper (`Productos. Productos del catálogo.`).
- Write clever headlines. Clarity over cleverness.

## Edit in four passes

1. Purposeful — does this help the operator decide?
2. Concise — cut every word that does not earn its place.
3. Conversational — would you say this out loud at the desk?
4. Clear — one meaning, no jargon the table does not already use.
